# Xiaohei Style

Inspired by Ian's open-source [ian-xiaohei-illustrations](https://github.com/helloianneo/ian-xiaohei-illustrations). Credit Ian's visual IP, guidelines, and illustration workflow. This repository adapts that language for stroke storytelling and does not claim originality. Always call the preset Xiaohei.

It suits abstract methods, systems, work states, and counterintuitive ideas, not biographies requiring accurate likenesses.

Request Xiaohei style and separately choose single scene, multiple scenes, or dual semantic islands. It is a visual style within stroke storytelling, not a third production route.

## Visual DNA

- Pure white or very pale warm-white, at least about 35% natural negative space.
- Slightly wobbly, sparse black lines; no pencil noise, dense hatching, or heavy shadows.
- Small black creature, white-dot eyes, simple limbs, restrained expression.
- One absurd but explanatory core action, not a collage.
- Sparse red, orange, and blue arrows, circles, stickers, or highlights.
- Generate precise keywords, numbers, and titles in deterministic post-production text layers.

## Image prompt framework

```text
16:9 minimalist hand-drawn editorial illustration on a pure white background.
One small black creature with tiny white dot eyes is [core action].
Use sparse slightly wobbly black ink lines, large clean negative space,
and only a few red, orange, and blue annotation accents.
The action must clearly express [concept/conflict]. Low line density, flat shapes,
no long text, no pseudo-writing, no photorealism, no glossy 3D, no dense texture.
Keep the lower 18% clear for subtitles and preserve a 6%-10% vertical gutter
when the scene contains left and right semantic islands.
```

## Stroke animation adaptation

- Each region carries one action or conclusion; dual islands draw left to right with no cross-island background lines.
- Large solid black fills may appear abruptly after outlines; reduce the subject size or split regions.
- Reveal keywords after the main contours, not before the conclusion.
- Do not substitute the Xiaohei mascot for a real person's likeness; use natural-skin-tone chibi guidelines.

## Avoid

Children's picture-book styling, commercial flat illustration, slide icon grids, black mascot silhouettes representing real people, crowded sticky notes/cards/decorative lines, and compositions understandable only through text.
